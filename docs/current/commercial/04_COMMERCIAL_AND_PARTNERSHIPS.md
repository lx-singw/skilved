# Commercial strategy and partnerships

Date: 11 September 2026  
Status: Recommended commercial programme; no paying demand, signed partnership or procurement award established by the interview.  
Owner: Founder / commercial lead.  
Related: [product strategy](../product/01_PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md), [cost and capacity](../operations/12_COST_CAPACITY_AND_OPERATIONS.md), [evidence register](../research/13_RESEARCH_AND_EVIDENCE.md).

## 1. Commercial objective

Update, 12 September 2026: the consumer tier is 100% free. The dedicated [TVET, SDF and employer business model](03_TVET_SDF_AND_EMPLOYER_BUSINESS_MODEL.md) prioritises institutional workflow hypotheses, distinguishes buyers from champions, separates grant processes and records current primary evidence. It supersedes earlier optional applicant-paid assistance proposals.

Build a useful public career product that can generate sustainable income through clearly valuable services and software.

Skilved must remain useful before employers, schools or government agencies join. Opportunity discovery, brought-in links, preparation and a living record give consumers independent value.

A small paid coordinator service is an early route to evidence and cash, not the definition of the company or the only permitted route to launch.

Interest from apprentice peers and school leavers is a starting audience signal. Their ability or willingness to pay is untested.

A commercial milestone requires an actual commitment or transaction with evidence. Forecasts do not become traction.

## 2. Who uses, chooses and pays

| Segment | User need | Possible buyer | What must be established |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual applicant | Understand opportunities and prepare efficiently | No consumer charge; organisations fund institutional services and may sponsor delivery | Useful free service, sustainable operating cost and independent record access |
| School/college cohort | Organise next steps and appropriate support | School, programme, funder or responsible organisation | Budget owner, consent responsibilities and recurring workload |
| Training programme | Resolve missing evidence and follow participant progress | Programme operator or training provider | Actual staff effort, reporting needs and authority to purchase |
| Employer | Receive useful, well-explained evidence and support onboarding | Recruitment, learning or programme team | Acceptance of records, workflow fit and measurable benefit |
| Assessor/RPL provider | Receive organised evidence against requirements | Provider or sponsoring organisation | Assessment process, authority and integration value |
| Government/public body | A specific programme or reporting need | Authorised procuring body | Legal access, procurement route, methodology and payment terms |
| Platform partner | Better preparation, referrals or records | Partner for a defined integration/service | Mutual benefit, data terms and technical ownership |

Do not assume that the person who likes the demonstration controls money. Record user, champion, budget owner, procurement owner and operational owner separately.

## 3. Revenue sequence

| Release | Offer hypothesis | Value to demonstrate | Readiness condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1–R2 alongside product | Bounded hands-on preparation/coordinator pilot | Less chasing and clearer missing-item work | Specific customer problem, scope, authorised inputs and capacity |
| R2–R3 | Organisation-funded preparation or review | Useful improvement to a real cohort's application/evidence packs | Quality process, buyer agreement and positive delivery economics; learners pay nothing |
| R3 | Organisation/cohort workspace | Reusable workflow and permissioned progress visibility | Repeat use and a buyer willing to fund continued access |
| R3–R4 | Assessed project sponsorship | Relevant learning evidence and feedback | Credible brief, reviewers and participant benefit |
| R4 | Programme lifecycle software | Onboarding, progress, evidence and handover | Contracted workflow and service expectations |
| R4 | Institution/assessor integration | Evidence receipt, correction and portability | Named recipient use and reliable integration |
| R5 conditional | Aggregated research or API service | A defined analytical or interoperability need | Suitable evidence, permissions, privacy and support economics |

These options are hypotheses, not simultaneous day-one businesses. Select the next offer from customer evidence and implementation dependencies.

Avoid funding the model through selling private career histories or obscuring commercial influence on recommendations.

## 4. Confirmed free consumer policy

Public listing details and original application routes remain free to access.

Access to a person's own record, meaningful export and deletion must not require a premium subscription.

All consumer functions are free; no optional applicant-paid assistance, premium matching, subscription or application charge is planned. Do not promise selection, a bursary, a job, qualification recognition or an employer response.

A sponsorship can pay for a cohort's access without making the sponsor owner of each participant's career identity.

Do not price or upgrade automatically because a person appears desperate, unemployed or near a deadline.

Institutional paid workflows should be tested for usefulness, buyer authority, willingness to pay and delivery cost. Free consumer use has transparent capacity/fair-use controls without learner payment demands.

The founder has not adopted an institutional price list. The old R200–R500 worker subscription and optional applicant-paid preparation are retired.

## 5. First paid service experiment

The existing pilot offers up to ten learner records, five agreed checks, two consolidated follow-up lists and a final progress report.

The introductory proposal is R1,500, split into R750 deposit and R750 on accepted deliverables, over seven working days after scope and inputs are ready.

This is an existing experimental offer, not researched market pricing or a commitment to deliver before capacity and customer agreement.

The customer coordinator manages learner communication unless another specific arrangement is agreed. Original sensitive records remain customer-controlled where sufficient.

The service can operate with the customer's existing register and approved tools. A production software deployment is not required to sell the experiment.

### What the experiment must answer

- Does the customer have an active, costly record-completion problem?
- Can Linda reduce administrative work without taking over assessment decisions?
- Is the final output useful in the customer's actual process?
- What proportion of delivery time is review, chasing, correction and coordination?
- Does the customer pay, accept the work and request repeat delivery?
- Which recurring steps justify software automation?

### Commercial record

Track interested, discovery completed, scope requested, scope agreed, invoice/deposit requested, deposit received, delivered, accepted, balance received and renewed separately.

Do not count a friendly conversation, letter of interest or sent invoice as revenue.

Retain the [original offer](../../archive/2026-09-11-pre-strategy-refresh/docs/first-paid-pilot/01_OFFER.md), [fictional demonstration](../../archive/2026-09-11-pre-strategy-refresh/docs/first-paid-pilot/02_DEMONSTRATION.md) and [delivery playbook](../../archive/2026-09-11-pre-strategy-refresh/docs/first-paid-pilot/03_DELIVERY_PLAYBOOK.md) as the starting experiment.

## 6. Public-product commercial experiments

### Consumer preparation

Observe a real application before and after preparation support. Learners pay nothing. Test whether an organisation will fund a defined cohort workflow with an actual budget and authorised data scope.

Compare the result with free current tools and a helpful person. If users cannot identify sufficient additional value, revise the offer.

### Organisation workspace

After repeated use, test a subscription or cohort fee covering named participants, workflows and support.

Measure staff hours, correction rates, task resolution and reuse. Do not assume an organisation buys because a student likes the app.

### Sponsored practical projects

A sponsor funds a useful, bounded project with a published brief and credible feedback. Participants know how their work will be used.

Distinguish learning exercises from productive commercial work; terms and participant interests require review before offering real-work programmes.

### Programme continuity

Test software for one existing programme's onboarding and progress flow rather than proposing to replace its entire system.

Demonstrate useful handovers and evidence updates across a full cycle before adding broad reporting commitments.

## 7. Partnership propositions to validate

| Potential relationship | Complementary role for Skilved | First concrete test | Do not imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harambee / SA Youth | Preparation, selected evidence and contextual referral where useful | Ask an appropriate team to review one workflow and identify actual gaps | That matching/readiness is absent from their service |
| MyMzansi / public infrastructure | Authorised identity or credential interoperability when available | Establish official access, terms and a named supported use case | Existing developer access or Skilved integration |
| Schools and TVET colleges | Supervised next steps, evidence and learner transition | Observe one coordinator workflow and one learner journey | Automatic authority to process pupil documents |
| Training providers | Record completeness, progress and participant handover | Deliver and measure an agreed pilot | Accreditation or assessment authority |
| Employers | Receipt of meaningful work/evidence and onboarding continuity | Have a reviewer use a sample pack for a defined task | Endorsement based on a listing scraped from their site |
| SETAs and public programme bodies | A specific funded workflow or reporting problem | Confirm mandate, buyer, procurement and required evidence | A contract, logo permission or a universal procurement threshold |
| Assessors/RPL providers | Organised portfolio and evidence-gap preparation | Review a provider's requirements and one consented portfolio | Guaranteed credit, recognition or qualification |
| Opportunity publishers | Attributed permitted discovery and accurate handoff | Agree feed/use terms and correction process | Blanket permission from public availability |
| NGOs and community groups | Practical support for an existing cohort | Co-design a small help/referral process | A duty to deliver unlimited support |
| Credential issuers/recipients | Portable records that retain provenance | Test an actual exchange and verification task | Certification merely from implementing a format |

Harambee publicly describes free SA Youth opportunity access, work-readiness resources and human support. These are overlapping capabilities, not a blank space for Skilved to claim. ## 8. Partnership development stages

1. Research the actual service, current official contact route and possible overlap.
2. Speak to the workflow owner about a concrete problem.
3. Show an honest demonstration with synthetic data.
4. Agree a bounded test and the minimum necessary data flow.
5. Measure actual benefit and operational burden.
6. Negotiate continued use, responsibilities and commercial terms if warranted.
7. Announce only what the organisation has authorised.

Maintain status as prospect, conversation, scoped experiment, agreed pilot, active relationship, paused or ended. A source in the catalogue is not a partner.

Do not use partner logos, imply preferred access or publish success stories without appropriate permission.

## 9. Procurement and delivery readiness

For a real purchase, establish the supplier identity, invoicing route, payment terms, applicable taxes and required customer paperwork with appropriate professional support.

For public bodies, confirm the institution's actual procurement process and governing requirements. The archived monetary thresholds are not a current legal guide.

No roadmap revenue depends on a tender award or a rapid institutional buying cycle.

A contract or pilot scope should identify deliverables, acceptance, exclusions, data roles, support windows, correction process and termination.

Technical integration is scoped separately when it adds credentials, migration, ongoing maintenance or third-party fees.

The founder's apprenticeship limits live availability. Publish support that can actually be delivered; additional operating capacity is estimated before expansion.

## 10. Unit economics and capacity

For each offer, calculate revenue collected, direct delivery costs, acquisition effort, delivery hours, support hours and correction/rework.

Gross contribution before founder labour equals collected revenue minus direct cash costs. It is not personal profit or spendable disposable income.

Effective delivery return must include sales and administration time when comparing offers.

Software economics should include model calls, source retrieval, compute, database, storage/downloads, messaging, payments, support and refunds where relevant.

Distinguish recurring revenue from one-off setup/pilot receipts. Do not multiply a one-week trial into annual recurring revenue.

Use the scenarios in [cost and operations](../operations/12_COST_CAPACITY_AND_OPERATIONS.md); no separate fixed spending ceiling is introduced here.

## 11. Trust and commercial independence

Sponsor or employer payment must not purchase a false qualification label, conceal a listing problem or alter recorded outcomes.

Any promoted opportunity is labelled and passes the same publishing requirements.

A paid organisation receives only the data participants have appropriately shared for the defined process.

Declining a paid service must not prevent a person from retrieving their own record or following the original free application route.

If employer payments create a conflict with an applicant's interests, disclose the arrangement and separate the relevant decision.

## 12. Expansion and financing

Repeatable revenue, accepted evidence and low-friction operations can support investment discussions, but they do not guarantee fundraising or valuation.

Estimate funding for a concrete release and operating period from the roadmap rather than promising national scale from a small initial contribution.

Record grants, credits, loans and equity separately; credits are temporary resources, not enduring unit economics.

Any future cofounder, employee or investor arrangement requires a clear role and terms. Personal relationships do not replace commercial clarity.

International offers must validate local buyers, currency/payment collection, qualification context and operating responsibilities before launch promises.

## 13. Commercial decision gates

Continue an offer when actual use, paid commitment and delivery economics justify another iteration.

Revise an offer when interest is high but the specific buyer, urgency or deliverable is wrong.

Pause expansion when support, source quality or privacy operations cannot keep up with the promised service.

Do not interpret failed pricing or one declined prospect as proof that the whole career platform cannot work; identify which hypothesis failed.

The full product roadmap continues alongside deliberately scoped experiments, with evidence deciding which commercial surfaces receive more investment.
