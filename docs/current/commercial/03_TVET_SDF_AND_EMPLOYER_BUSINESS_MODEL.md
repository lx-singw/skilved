## 1. Business model decision

**The consumer tier is 100% free. Organisations pay for programme administration, evidence preparation, coordination and reporting.** Remove the historical R200–R500 worker subscription and the later optional applicant-paid preparation proposal from current plans. No paid learner upgrades, matching fees, application charges, outcome commissions or paywall for career-record access are proposed.

Free does not mean unlimited compute or guaranteed human support. Use transparent fair-use controls, efficient shared processing and funded service capacity without converting exhausted usage into a learner payment demand. Institutional sponsorship cannot make the sponsor owner of the learner's whole record or permit the product fee to be passed to work seekers. Keep personal records portable after a programme ends.

Section 15(1) of the Employment Services Act prohibits charging work seekers for employment services; the Act also contains a mechanism for specified exceptions. This is a service-classification issue, not a universal ban on every software subscription. Skilved is not planning to rely on an exception. Organisation payment does not by itself resolve any agency-registration or other applicable obligations. [Employment Services Act, section 15](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/Public%20Employment%20Services/Employment%20Services%20Act%202014.pdf)

Affordability is a strong design constraint for the intended audience. Do not assert that every unemployed person can never pay, assume every person receives a grant, or use an unverified current grant amount as market evidence. The free-consumer decision stands independently of those generalisations.

## 2. Product focus

Same-day research update: integrated learner-management and specialist evidence products also occupy this market. Before committing to a paid build, complete the relevant [strategic investigations](../research/02_STRATEGIC_INVESTIGATIONS_AND_PRODUCT_OPTIONS.md), especially installed alternatives, recent failures, recipient acceptance and buyer authority. Treat administration/reporting as a problem category to investigate, not an uncontested niche. The most promising complement may be resolving evidence and handover problems around the customer's existing system; that remains a hypothesis.

The consumer experience is a personal career copilot: establish the opportunity source, check requirements against supplied facts, organise evidence, explain missing items, prepare a selected package and follow useful next steps. The continuing career record remains central; this is not a pivot to a college-only compliance database.

All six public MVP categories remain: bursaries, learnerships, apprenticeships, internships, graduate programmes and jobs. Broad opportunity coverage supplies this experience; listing volume alone is not the differentiation thesis. The institutional product reuses permissioned evidence and task machinery for a specific operational workflow.

Harambee's June 2026 article describes a network reaching more than 5.1 million young people and discusses the SA Youth CV. That is its reported reach, not a verified active-user or registered-account count for this study. Do not claim that career records or preparation are absent from its offering, or that Skilved is mathematically certain to fail because of its scale. [Harambee, June 2026](https://www.harambee.co.za/beyond-the-storm-transforming-the-youth-employment-landscape-through-data-and-bold-action/)

Potential differentiation to prove: fewer missing or inconsistent records, faster preparation, recipient-accepted exports, accurate placement/milestone evidence and less coordinator effort. Partnerships should improve an existing platform or programme workflow without requiring replacement of its system.

## 3. Buyer, user and approver are different

| Segment | Day-to-day user / champion | Candidate paying entity and approver | First workflow hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| TVET college | Placement/WIL office, student support, programme coordinator | College through its actual delegated budget/procurement process, or an authorised programme funder | Placement evidence register, missing-item follow-up and recipient-required report |
| Lead employer / funded programme operator | Programme administrator, SDF, learning and development team | Employer/operator budget owner with finance/procurement approval | One programme's milestone evidence and claim-preparation pack |
| Independent SDF practice | SDF/consultants managing employer accounts | Consultancy owner, where client agreements allow processing and sufficient recurring value exists | Isolated client workspaces and reusable evidence follow-up |
| Skills development provider | Learner administration, programme/quality staff | Provider management or programme sponsor | Learner onboarding/completion evidence and accepted handover |
| SETA itself | Programme, monitoring or grants staff | SETA through an appropriate actual procurement process | Later integration/reporting need, after demand and access are evidenced |

An SDF may be an employer employee or an external practitioner. SASSETA describes the SDF as an organisation-nominated liaison who assists with WSP/ATR and related requirements; this does not make every SDF a SETA employee or budget holder. [SASSETA SDF role](https://www.sasseta.org.za/employers/skills-development-facilitators/)

Do not assume the placement officer can buy, the SDF can sign, or a grant automatically permits software expenditure. Establish champion, contracting entity, budget, delegated authority, procurement/security requirements, payment terms and data authority separately.

## 5. Three workflows, developed separately

### A. TVET placement evidence

Import an authorised roster → bind programme/cohort and reporting period → record candidate preparation and placement status → request the required evidence → obtain an attributable host/supervisor confirmation where applicable → reconcile missing/contradictory records → export the recipient's required listing and evidence index.

Keep looking, prepared, referred, offered, started, in progress, completed and unknown distinct. A referral or offer is not proof of a start. Preserve the recipient's precise indicator definition rather than inflating placement totals. A dashboard cannot create employer places that do not exist; test whether the office's main bottleneck is evidence administration, available hosts or something else before selling the former as the solution to both.

### B. Discretionary-grant programme evidence

An authorised programme owner supplies the relevant agreement, annexures and instructions. Draft a requirement template with exact source clauses; a responsible human approves it. Map each requirement to programme/milestone, applicability, evidence type, deadline, reviewer and permissible retention. Track missing items and discrepancies, then prepare a versioned claim-support pack for the responsible signatory.

Contracts, certified-copy requirements, attendance and stipend/payment evidence are included only when applicable. Do not hardcode all of them as universal requirements. The system can flag a missing signature or inconsistent date; it cannot certify a copy, authenticate attendance or establish genuine delivery from a PDF alone. No automated fabrication, backdating or signature application is allowed.

A pack's completeness is measured against its versioned approved template. Keep completeness, authenticity, authorised review, submission receipt and funder acceptance separate. Signed-off and submitted snapshots are immutable; later corrections create new versions with context.

### C. Mandatory-grant WSP/ATR assistance

Treat this as a separate discovery and later product scope: workforce/training planning and reporting needs are different from one learner's milestone pack. Establish the relevant fields, planning cycle, consultation/sign-off requirements and official submission system before implementation. Do not promise a full WSP/ATR system merely because the buyer is an SDF.

## 6. First institutional product slice

Build one shared organisation workspace with scoped roles, a reviewed CSV/roster import, versioned requirement templates, a learner-by-requirement exception view, ownership of follow-up tasks, evidence provenance, selected secure access, a review/approval receipt and an export with an evidence index. Configure either a TVET cohort or one employer programme from actual customer requirements first.

Add organisation identity, programme and grant/reporting period to every relevant rule and export. A consultant switching clients must change tenant context; client A cannot search client B's records. A programme record retained under a justified institutional obligation is distinct from the person's independently controlled career record. Explain retained copies and purposes; payment alone cannot override user privacy.

No direct SETA write integration, full student-information-system replacement, payroll, biometric attendance, automatic certified-document verification or blanket cross-SETA rules engine is included in this first slice. Interoperable CSV/PDF and traceable handoff can establish value before an API agreement.

## 7. Pricing structure to test

The learner pays R0. Test an organisation-funded plan with a clear workspace/programme allowance, active-cohort capacity, staff roles, support coverage and term. Quote onboarding/template configuration separately when it creates real work. A larger institution may later require an annual agreement; a small SDF practice may prefer a shorter recurring term. None of these prices or preferences is validated yet.

Set the price using observed delivery/support cost and conservative buyer value. An illustrative value worksheet is staff hours demonstrably saved × the buyer's agreed loaded hourly cost, plus separately evidenced reduction in rework. Compare this with setup and recurring price. Exclude hypothetical recovered grants, imagined placements and duplicated savings. Track direct cost, founder/service labour and payment collection.

The existing R1,500 ten-record pilot is an optional organisation-paid discovery experiment, not a college-wide licence price or a permanent manual-service business model. Use a repeatable demonstration and a bounded paid design-partner engagement to validate software requirements. Repeated custom document chasing without reusable workflow or renewal evidence is a signal to revise the approach.

## 8. Customer acquisition and proof

Prioritise access to a real operating workflow and an identifiable budget owner. In parallel, interview a TVET placement office, an employer/programme operator and an SDF practice. This is a research sequence, not a claim that all three will buy. A smaller private operator may have a shorter decision path; verify that assumption rather than treating public colleges as inherently slow or inaccessible.

For each prospect obtain: the last report/claim cycle, a redacted rejected or incomplete example where available, existing system/process, manual hours, actual consequence, required output, authority over records, budget owner and next decision date. Build the demo from the agreed template using synthetic or authorised redacted material. Do not send unsolicited outreach from this documentation task.

Run one complete reporting/milestone cycle with baseline and agreed acceptance. Measure time to resolve missing items, rework, report acceptance, support time and actual paid commitment. Reuse the same core workflow for a second cycle/customer before expanding integrations. A compliance obligation establishes a problem context; only adoption, payment and repeat value establish Skilved's market.

A partner/channel arrangement with an SDF practice requires client-by-client authority and appropriate commercial/data terms. Government programmes, SETA forums and colleges can be discovery or referral channels; no endorsement is assumed. Institutional sales still require demonstration, trust, procurement and follow-up; enterprise positioning does not eliminate customer validation.

## 9. Roadmap and acceptance

Use S01/S06/S11 to investigate both user preparation and the named institutional workflows without delaying the six-category public MVP. S15 implements the first coordinator workspace/template pilot; S16 establishes organisation-funded plans/billing; S18/S20 deepen programme/milestone records. Direct reporting integrations remain later and access-dependent. WSP/ATR automation requires separate validated scope and re-estimation.

Before releasing the institutional slice, demonstrate: cross-tenant denial; correct source/version/application of requirements; missing versus reviewed/authenticated distinctions; no duplicate counting across milestones; accessible learner actions; a recipient-usable export; proper signatory review; no learner charging; documented retention/access; and an agreed buyer acceptance outcome. Use existing PRD-024, 043, 050–053 and 062 as the starting mapping. Material additions revise inclusive estimates rather than silently keeping the same delivery promise.
