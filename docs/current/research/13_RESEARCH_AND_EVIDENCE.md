## 1. Evidence classes

| Label | Meaning | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed founder direction | Direct statement in the conversation | A product intent or preference, not demonstrated demand |
| Founder-reported interest | Founder spoke to apprentice peers and school leavers who expressed interest | Accessible first audience and a discovery lead; no fabricated interviews, counts or commitments |
| Repository observation | Source/document exists or behaviour was actually inspected | Only the observed implementation state; a plan or stored report is not live verification |
| Public primary-source fact | Official source describes its product, policy, standard or rules | Evidence of that specific statement at the access date, with limits |
| Product hypothesis | Proposed value, differentiator, retention mechanism or buyer | Requires a defined experiment and decision rule |
| Measured result | Reproducible observation with method, sample, date and artefact | Limited to the tested population and conditions |
| External dependency | Access, permission, agreement or service not obtained | Must remain conditional in the roadmap |

No probability of startup success, universal competitor score, guaranteed virality or future revenue can be inferred from the material collected so far. Aspirations to lead the market become measurable benchmark objectives rather than advertising claims.

## 7. What the evidence does and does not justify

Supported direction: portable progress, actionable opportunity requirements, structured assistance, coordinator workflows, evidence provenance and eventual interoperable records can be designed as a connected experience. The six opportunity categories and the ambition to reach school learners remain founder requirements.

Unproven differentiation: better completion rates, lower coordinator effort, trustworthy career evidence accepted by organisations, repeat weekly use, a productive social network, high-quality partner referrals, willingness to pay and defensible outcome intelligence. These require task observation and experiments, not more confident wording.

Potential moat: accumulated trusted relationships and accepted evidence, effective assistance, repeat organisational use and carefully governed learning from outcomes. This is a hypothesis. A database, agent count, scraping volume or a social feed is not proof of a moat.

## 9. Maintenance and claim publication

Before publishing a comparison, rerun relevant product tasks and check current source terms. Archive the permitted evidence, benchmark dataset/version and evaluation method. Record failures and disadvantages as well as wins. A source changes status when its page changes or cannot be reached; a stale citation must not support a current universal claim.

Each new entry requires ID, question, source/artefact, access date, observation date if different, bounded finding, evidence class, limitations, owner and recheck trigger. Retain participant notes and consent in an appropriately restricted research store, not in this public code repository. Public documentation uses anonymised findings that cannot reasonably identify individuals.

Success claims must identify the task, population, comparison, metric and date. Until measurements exist, write "we aim to", "we propose" or "we are testing". The aspiration to excel is retained; superiority is earned through evidence.
