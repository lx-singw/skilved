## The questions to answer through observation

The founder does not have to guess answers to these questions before progress. They become planned work with an owner and an evidence artifact.

| ID | Question | Method | Useful artifact / decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| EXP-01 | What actually causes an applicant to stop? | Observe a recent real application from each represented category; record obstacles without coaching first | Task map with timestamps, errors and source documents |
| EXP-02 | Does reusable evidence save effort on the second opportunity? | Have the same consenting user prepare two suitable applications; record rework and correctness | Comparison of first and subsequent preparation |
| EXP-03 | Is the checklist understandable and accurate? | Independent source-based review plus user think-aloud trial | Incorrect mandatory requirements block release |
| EXP-04 | Does a user return when the next step becomes due? | Follow a chosen goal for four weeks; distinguish voluntary return, prompted useful action and passive opens | Stage-specific retention log |
| EXP-05 | Can structured help outperform an ordinary group message? | Compare matched tasks using the existing coordinator process and Skilved; alternate order where feasible | Resolution time and coordinator minutes, not message volume |
| EXP-06 | Does a receiving person value the evidence? | Two or more independent recipients inspect a real consented pack and explain what they can use | Acceptance constraints and requested changes |
| EXP-07 | Who can buy the coordinator workflow? | Interview user, budget owner and approver separately; present concrete deliverable and experimental price | Written scope, decision date, actual invoice/payment if agreed |
| EXP-08 | Can delivery support a margin? | Log founder/service hours, infrastructure, communication and corrections on the pilot | Contribution margin with founder time shown separately |
| EXP-09 | Which social action creates additional utility? | Invite a referee/teammate for a specific action; track useful completion and recipient consent | Contribution rate and downstream user benefit |
| EXP-10 | What happens after acceptance or rejection? | Interview users at each outcome and offer a next-step workflow only when wanted | Validated transition needs, not forced engagement |
| EXP-11 | Can under-18 participation be operated responsibly? | Establish partner/guardian process, controlled contact, reporting and staff ownership; test with suitable supervision | Safeguarding and permission readiness assessment |
| EXP-12 | What can be operated within real capacity? | Log two iterations, including source review/support and unplanned work | Re-estimated throughput and release window |
| EXP-13 | What does one active user and one opportunity cost? | Instrument real bounded workloads and account for source failures/model retries | Cost ledger and cost-to-serve distribution |
| EXP-14 | Does the proposed advantage survive a fair comparison? | Follow the benchmark protocol with relevant alternatives, same tasks and permitted access | Dated results with sample/limits; no invented competitor scores |

## Suggested interview script for first users

1. Tell me about the last opportunity you wanted to pursue. Show the actual notice if comfortable.
2. What did you do first? What happened next? Where did you stop or need help?
3. Which information did you have to recreate, and who helped you?
4. What would have happened without that person or tool?
5. Which parts of your work or experience do you struggle to demonstrate?
6. Who has accepted that evidence before? What did they ask to see?
7. Try this small task in Skilved without my instructions. Explain what you expect each action to do.
8. What would make you stop using it? What do you want to keep private?
9. May we follow up about this same goal at an agreed time? Participation is optional.

Do not lead with whether they like AI, would join a revolutionary platform or think the founder will succeed. Observe behaviour and inspect completed work when consented. Praise and interest are useful context but weak demand evidence.

## Interpretation and decision rules

- Small pilots reveal friction; they do not establish national effect sizes or prove causality.
- If people use opportunities but ignore evidence, investigate the transition to their second application before discarding the core record idea.
- If recipients cannot interpret evidence, improve its content and format before adding verification branding.
- If groups create conversation without resolving work, reduce the social surface or change its purpose.
- If coordinators save time but cannot pay, test the budget owner and procurement path rather than assume a subscription market.
- If a free tool already solves the observed problem well, identify a different unmet step; do not manufacture a comparison advantage.
- If service revenue appears, distinguish founder labour revenue from repeatable software revenue.
- The outcome can be proceed, revise, seek a dependency, defer a feature or stop a specific experiment. No result guarantees the whole company will succeed or fail.

## Records and privacy

Use pseudonymous research IDs and explicit permission for recordings, work samples and follow-up. Keep identifying interview notes outside public repository documents. Publish anonymised summaries only after checking that combinations of details do not identify people. Do not publish the founder's private relationship history, contact lists or household budget as startup documentation.
