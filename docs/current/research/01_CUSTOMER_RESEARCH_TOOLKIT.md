# Customer research and experiment toolkit

13 September sequence: use this toolkit for brief launch-relevant task checks during M0, then start the broader strategic programme under the [post-M0 schedule](../product/03_DISCOVERY_FIRST_MVP.md). Buyer/recipient research does not block discovery launch; it remains necessary before the corresponding institutional product commitment.

Date: 11 September 2026. Status: proposed protocol. Existing evidence is expressed interest from apprentice peers and school leavers; the studies below have not been completed.

## Research questions

Prioritisation update, 12 September 2026: the [strategic investigation register](02_STRATEGIC_INVESTIGATIONS_AND_PRODUCT_OPTIONS.md) defines INV-01–18, the artefacts to collect and the decisions each could change. It adds installed institutional systems, actual recipient acceptance, participant burden, host capacity and procurement economics to the original questions. Desk research is not a substitute for observing these workflows.

Which recent task failed or took unnecessary effort? What evidence does the person already have? Who needs to accept it? What would make the next visit useful? Which missing information prevents action? What does a coordinator currently do in WhatsApp, spreadsheets or Drive? Who pays for the problem, and what would they buy?

Research must distinguish user desirability, recipient acceptance, buyer demand and operational feasibility. One enthusiastic participant cannot answer all four.

## Interview guide

Begin with the last concrete opportunity or programme milestone. Ask the participant to recount what they did, where they found information, what files they used, who helped and what happened. Explore confusion, repeated typing, missing documents, transport/data barriers and whether a deadline or requirement changed. Ask what they would do without Skilved. Avoid leading with a product pitch or asking them to predict years of usage.

For coordinators, ask about the most recent cohort, missing-record follow-up, reporting/handover recipient, existing tools, time spent, error consequences and procurement. Ask who can authorise access and payment. For employers/assessors, show a selected evidence pack and ask which claims they can use, what is missing and what would cause rejection.

## Observation protocol

Explain purpose, voluntary participation, what is recorded, retention and withdrawal route. Use an appropriate process for minors; do not recruit under-18 private/social trials before safeguarding readiness. Prefer synthetic/redacted examples where sensitive data is unnecessary. Do not record secretly or collect complete identity documents for research convenience.

Give the participant a realistic task without coaching. Record success, assistance, misunderstanding, time range and the actual next action. Quote only with permission and preserve context. Separate observed behaviour from researcher interpretation.

## Experiment cards

| ID | Hypothesis | Method | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|---|---|
| EXP-01 | A saved preparation workspace provides first-session value | Observe users preparing a real/plausible opportunity | Completed useful step and accurate explanation of next action |
| EXP-02 | Relevant changes create repeat use | Two-to-four-week opt-in diary/usage trial | Voluntary return to resolve a task; notification dependence and seasonality |
| EXP-03 | Evidence packs are accepted outside Skilved | Ask actual recipients to use selected packs | Acceptance, missing elements, verification burden and need for another format |
| EXP-04 | Attributable activity matters before formal employment | Compare task completion with/without relevant activity evidence | Recipient usefulness, user effort and claim clarity |
| EXP-05 | Structured help resolves blockers | Small invited group with appropriate safeguards | Resolved requests, time/help burden, unwanted contact |
| EXP-06 | Coordinator will pay for reduced follow-up | Present the scoped pilot with an actual price | Accepted terms, payment, delivery effort and repeat request |
| EXP-07 | Hybrid ingestion is operationally sustainable | Curate across all six categories for a defined period | Accepted records, critical errors, source permissions and minutes per record |
| EXP-08 | Agents add value over manual reminders | Compare equivalent tasks with/without bounded automation | Useful actions, errors, cancellations, cost and user understanding |

Sample sizes and decision thresholds are specified before each trial according to available users and the question. A small formative sample identifies problems; it cannot establish national demand or causal employment impact. Use [benchmarks](05_COMPETITIVE_BENCHMARKS.md) for fair comparative experiments.

## Diary prompts

Ask when a relevant event occurs or at an agreed short interval: what career task arose, what tool was used, what changed, whether Skilved helped, what remained blocked and whether another person was involved. Do not pressure participants to create activity just to satisfy weekly retention. A bursary season can create episodic use that is still valuable.

## Analysis and decisions

Maintain an evidence table: participant segment, task/context, observation, quote permission, interpretation, contradictory evidence, confidence, proposed action and next check. Include non-users, abandoned tasks and recipients who rejected the pack. Do not count friends' politeness as payment intent.

After each round, choose a small set of consequential changes, explain evidence and retest. Preserve failed hypotheses to prevent repeating them as established strategy. Research outputs must change a product, operating or commercial decision; otherwise narrow the question.
