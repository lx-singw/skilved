## 1. What excellence means

The founder wants the best useful product and strong performance across relevant benchmarks. This is an improvement objective, not permission to declare Skilved universally best.

Evaluate correctness, usefulness, effort, accessibility, privacy, reliability, recipient acceptance, retention and economics together.

A fast response with an invented qualification is a failure. A persuasive demo with excessive founder intervention is not proof of a self-service product.

A competitive advantage is specific: a named audience completes a named task better under stated conditions. It can coexist with a competitor's superior reach, support or existing adoption.

## 2. Evidence levels

| Label | Meaning | Permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Officially described | Capability appears on a current primary page | The provider describes it; availability and performance still need testing |
| Observed in product | Reviewer used the feature in a recorded session | Worked in that account, market and version |
| Benchmark result | A preregistered comparative task was run | Result applies to the stated task and conditions |
| User outcome | An actual person achieved a recorded result | Evidence source and attribution limitations remain relevant |
| Hypothesis | Reasoned opportunity or proposed advantage | Needs testing; not a factual competitor gap |
| Unknown | Insufficient access or evidence | Not equivalent to absent |

Never turn an inaccessible competitor feature into a red cross in a comparison table.

For the paid workflow, compare **the customer's actual system and process** with **that same system plus Skilved** on the same permitted task. Include total effort for learner, supervisor, coordinator and recipient, migration/setup cost, wrong or missing facts, output acceptance and repeat use. Give both configurations equivalent access to useful training/configuration; do not handicap an installed product to manufacture a win. No additional benchmark results are claimed.

- Can users prepare more correctly and with less repetition than with their usual tools?
- Is there a recurring handover problem between a learner and a helper or institution?
- Does evidence produced through Skilved help a recipient make a better-informed review?
- Does persistent assistance resume appropriately when facts change?
- Does the first cohort prefer useful support enough to return voluntarily?
- Does supporting all six categories create useful continuity or confusing navigation?
- Is a paying organisation's problem sufficiently urgent and expensive to justify adoption?
- Can this quality be maintained at the cost and staffing available?

Competitor pages cannot answer these questions by themselves. They guide the test, not the conclusion.

## 5. Baseline configurations

### Baseline A: actual current practice

Observe the participant doing a relevant task with the tools and support they normally use.

Record existing familiarity, coordinator help, setup time, messages, mistakes and completion. Do not replace their competent helper with an intentionally weak substitute.

### Baseline B: organised common-tool workflow

Provide the same source material, private folder, checklist/register and a defined coordinator role.

Record all human effort. This asks whether Skilved improves on a reasonably organised process, not merely a disorganised one.

### Baseline C: single-session AI assistance

Provide the same approved source and user facts to a documented model/configuration, without private sensitive records unless the setup is authorised.

Use a competent prompt and allow normal corrections. Record model/version, date, input, output, costs, uncertainty handling and human review.

### Baseline D: relevant specialist product

Select a competitor only for tasks it actually supports in the tested account and market.

Use the relevant tier and normal documented workflow. Disclose login, geography, language, subscription and access limitations.

Do not penalise a simulation platform for not being an SA bursary database; compare its simulation/evidence workflow where relevant.

### Skilved configuration

Record build commit, database/source snapshot, model settings, enabled agent mandates, account role and any manual operator steps.

Report both the self-service result and operator-assisted result when intervention differs. Do not hide concierge work inside an automation score.

## 7. Fixture and reference-answer construction

Use current permitted sources or clearly labelled synthetic source snapshots. Preserve date, intake and source version.

A competent human reviewer builds a reference checklist of stated requirements and known unknowns.

Do not let the same model both generate the answer and act as the only judge of its correctness.

Where wording is ambiguous, record acceptable interpretations and the need for issuer confirmation rather than force one false ground truth.

Include absent deadlines, changed deadlines, conflicting sources, multiple locations, duplicate intakes and unavailable pages.

For evidence tasks, distinguish document presence, readability, reviewer observation and issuer verification.

Never submit real applications, message real contacts or expose a person's documents merely to complete a benchmark without the appropriate authorisation.

## 8. Measures and calculation rules

| Dimension | Measure | Calculation / recording rule |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement correctness | Correct extracted material conditions | Compare each condition and qualifier with reviewed source; report omissions and additions |
| Eligibility safety | False “meets requirement” statements | Count known disqualifying gaps incorrectly represented as met; unknowns are their own category |
| Catalogue coverage | Usable relevant listings in a declared source sample | Denominator is the inspected sample, not all opportunities in SA |
| Freshness | Correct current status at check time | Compare with the source snapshot and note uncertainty |
| Preparation usefulness | Correct user-understood next action | Observe user explanation and actual work rather than fluency alone |
| Efficiency | Task completion and human effort | Include setup, clarification, corrections and helper/operator time |
| Evidence quality | Traceability and recipient comprehension | Reviewer identifies activity, origin, assessor and limits |
| Reliability | Successful and duplicate-free continuation | Include retries, failures, blocked states and recovery time |
| Retention | Return that advances an active goal | Segment lifecycle, window and missingness |
| Accessibility | Completion on declared device/access setup | Record observed barriers; automated audit is supplementary |
| Cost | Cash cost and operating effort per completed useful task | Include failed attempts, source fetches, messaging and human work |
| Trust/safety | Unauthorized disclosure/action and serious misrepresentation | Report each incident; do not average it away with speed |
| Commercial usefulness | Paid continuation or accepted deliverable | Interest, invoice and payment are distinct states |

If a result cannot be observed, mark it unknown or not applicable with a reason. Do not insert an estimated score as a measurement.

## 9. Optional qualitative scoring

For comparable reviewed artefacts, a 0–4 rubric may help organise judgement:

- 0: Fails the task or contains a consequential error.
- 1: Substantial correction required before the user can proceed.
- 2: Partly useful, with material gaps clearly identified.
- 3: Correct and usable for the stated task with minor clarification.
- 4: Correct, understandable and efficient, with relevant uncertainty and next steps.

Define task-specific examples before judging. Keep individual dimensions visible.

Do not combine privacy failures, fabricated qualifications or unauthorised actions into a harmless-looking average.

A higher ordinal score in one small test does not establish market leadership or statistical significance.

## 10. Fairness and reproducibility

Use equivalent source facts, user information and task goals across configurations.

Record training/familiarisation so a founder expert in Skilved is not compared with a novice using another tool.

Counterbalance task order where practical because a first attempt teaches the participant what to do.

Blind artefact reviewers to the product name where feasible; UI observations cannot always be blinded and that limitation should be stated.

Include unsuccessful users and failed runs. Predefine exclusions such as unrelated connectivity loss and report them.

A paid tier may outperform a free tier; report cost and tier rather than hide the distinction.

An unavailable regional feature is a relevant coverage limitation, but not proof its underlying implementation is poor.

Re-run only when a meaningful change or unresolved defect warrants it; preserve earlier results rather than overwrite an unfavourable comparison.

## 11. Experiment record template

Each completed benchmark record contains:

1. Benchmark ID, question and preregistered success/failure interpretation.
2. Date, product/version/tier, source snapshot and participant context.
3. Permission and data-handling basis, using non-identifying participant IDs.
4. Task instructions, reference answer and rubric version.
5. Raw observations, timing, outputs, failures and human intervention.
6. Reviewer assessment, disagreement and resolved judgement.
7. Direct costs and counted human effort.
8. Result, uncertainty, exclusions and what cannot be concluded.
9. Decision, owner and next action.
10. A stable evidence link in the research register.

Use readable tables or structured records; private participant evidence must not be committed to a public repository.

## 12. Release application

R0 establishes fixture contracts and exposes mocked/hardcoded trust outcomes.

R1 uses observed preparation tasks and current-practice baselines to improve the first experience.

R2 evaluates all six categories, durability, export, privacy, accessibility and actual operating cost before public claims.

R3 adds help, groups and recipient evaluation with real helpers and moderation.

R4 validates institutional, programme and assessment workflows through named authorised recipients.

R5 evaluates analytical validity and country-specific usefulness; national or causal claims require more than passing a UI benchmark.

Exact release decisions and defect thresholds belong in [validation and release](../quality/11_VALIDATION_AND_RELEASE.md). This document defines how comparative evidence is obtained.

## 13. What may be said publicly

Acceptable: “In this dated test, these participants completed this task with these results; here is the method and limitation.”

Not acceptable: “No competitor does this,” “the first in Africa,” “guaranteed employment,” “95% of all applications automated,” or “best career platform” without adequate supporting evidence.

Publish source, sample and context beside the claim, and correct a claim when the supporting product or evidence changes.

Treat competitor marketing outcomes as the competitor's claims unless independently assessed; do not import their headline conversion multipliers into Skilved forecasts.
