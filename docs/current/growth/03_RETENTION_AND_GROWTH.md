# Retention, meaningful participation and growth

13 September sequence: [local distribution and career network](04_LOCAL_DISTRIBUTION_AND_CAREER_NETWORK.md) governs M0 sharing, useful returns and the preserved later social ambition. The richer career-progress measures below apply when those features exist; M0 route opens, shares and local self-reports cannot be presented as evidenced applications or placements. Trending follows genuine activity and an explicit experiment, with no adopted ten-browser threshold.

Date: 11 September 2026  
Status: Experiment programme; no retention or acquisition result has yet been established.  
Owner: Founder / product and growth.  
Related: [strategy](../product/01_PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md), [requirements](../product/02_PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md), [validation](../quality/11_VALIDATION_AND_RELEASE.md), [interview record](../research/19_FOUNDER_INTERVIEW_AND_EXPERIMENTS.md).

## 1. Retention thesis

People should return because Skilved helps them advance a goal, preserve useful evidence or support another person's next step.

A living record can support episodic use for years. An employed person who exports a record and returns months later for a transition may be receiving value even without weekly activity.

An opportunity feed is an entry point. Reliable preparation, useful help and accumulated evidence are the reasons to keep the relationship.

The founder's peer and school-leaver conversations establish interest only. This plan describes what to test before interpreting that interest as demand.

## 2. Candidate north-star measure

**Weekly people making meaningful career progress with recorded evidence**, segmented by active journey.

A meaningful progress event is a user-relevant change: resolving a requirement, preparing reviewed material, completing a real help request, supplying assessed work, recording an evidenced submission, or advancing a programme milestone.

The event includes the goal, action, actor, timestamp, supporting artefact or observation, and evidence class.

User-reported completion is useful but remains labelled user-reported. Platform action logs prove the platform action, not the underlying external career outcome.

A task created and immediately checked off without supporting work must not count merely because it produces two events.

Count a person once within the headline weekly measure; retain action totals only as diagnostics. Review samples for inflated or trivial completion.

Do not compare all users against one frequency requirement. Separate actively applying, preparing/learning, participating in a programme, employed/stable and paused cohorts.

## 3. Supporting measures

| Measure | Definition | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first useful next step | Time from entry to a user-understood relevant action with source/context | Fast generated output can still be wrong |
| Preparation completion | Opportunity workspaces meeting their user-selected preparation requirements | Does not mean accepted application |
| Help resolution | Requests the requester confirms resolved, with reason/evidence | Closed by moderator is not resolved |
| Record reuse | A relevant existing fact/evidence item used for another purpose with permission | Reopening a profile alone is not reuse |
| Return to progress | A return visit that advances an active goal | Notification click alone is not retention |
| Recipient usefulness | A recipient understands and uses selected evidence for a named task | Viewing a link is insufficient |
| Staff effort | Actual coordinator/founder time per supported person or case | Exclude neither setup nor corrections |
| Stage outcome | Award, start, completion or other outcome with evidence source | Association does not prove Skilved caused it |
| Voluntary referral | User initiates a share/invitation that helps another person begin | Invitations sent do not equal acquired users |
| Support burden | Time, incidents and unresolved requests per cohort | Growth can worsen quality even if signups rise |

Use fixed observation windows, disclose missing responses and preserve the denominator. Show actual counts beside percentages for small cohorts.

## 4. First-week experience to test

| Moment | Proposed experience | What we need to learn |
|---|---|---|
| First visit | Browse a useful opportunity or bring an existing link | Does the user recognise a current need? |
| First session | Choose a goal and obtain a source-linked next-step checklist | Can they explain what to do without the founder coaching? |
| First saved work | Reuse or enter only facts needed for that goal | Is the record valuable before it becomes complete? |
| First obstacle | Request targeted help or record the question to ask the issuer | Is the obstacle actually solvable through this product? |
| First return | Resume the next action with prior work preserved | Does continuity save time or reduce confusion? |
| First week review | Show completed work, remaining items and user-controlled reminders | Does the user feel clearer and want continued support? |

These moments are experience design, not promises that every user acts daily or completes an application in seven days.

Users arriving after a deadline should receive accurate closure and relevant next options, not manufactured urgency.

Do not gate the first useful step behind a long biography, full document vault, contact import or arbitrary profile-completeness percentage.

## 5. Stage-specific retention

### School exploration and school leaving

Help a person compare real routes, preserve genuine school/project evidence and prepare for dated application windows.

Return triggers can be newly available results, an opening intake, a relevant information session or feedback on a selected project.

Under-18 communication and group access follow the supervised pathway; acquisition targets cannot bypass safety readiness.

### Active applications

Useful triggers are material listing changes, a genuinely missing requirement, an approaching chosen deadline and an unresolved question.

A user can mark an opportunity irrelevant, pause a goal or reduce message frequency without losing records.

Avoid daily generic matches that ignore the person's constraints or reuse the same stale opportunity.

### Award or offer received

Celebrate only what the user chooses to share. Ask whether their next goal is acceptance, documents, registration, onboarding or deciding between options.

Switch the assistance to that goal rather than continuing to pressure them to apply elsewhere.

### Learning or programme participation

Offer milestones, evidence capture and appropriate check-ins chosen with the participant and programme.

Useful return may be monthly or event-driven. Lack of a login cannot become an automatic dropout label.

### Stable employment

Make the record easy to update after a meaningful responsibility, project, training event or reference.

Respect quieter periods. Do not imply a successful user should remain anxious about finding the next role.

### Re-entry and transition

Preserve exportable history and let a returning user review what changed. Do not require starting their career profile again.

Renew expiring consent, confirm facts and suggest only the next relevant workflow.

## 6. Retention experiments

| ID | Release | Hypothesis | Comparison / observation | Decision signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RET-01 | R1 | A specific checklist is more useful than a listing plus generic advice | Same opportunity, observed preparation with current tools and with Skilved | Correct next step, missing-item detection, time and user preference |
| RET-02 | R1–R2 | Saved context brings people back to continue | Observe voluntary return to a real unfinished goal | Work resumed with less repetition, not merely sessions |
| RET-03 | R2 | Material-change alerts outperform generic daily messages | Consented cohort comparison with matched relevance where feasible | More useful actions per message and fewer unwanted messages |
| RET-04 | R2–R3 | Reusable evidence reduces repeated preparation | First and second application, controlling for different requirements | Less re-entry with equal or better correctness |
| RET-05 | R3 | A structured request receives more useful help | Compare usual group question with structured request using willing helpers | Resolution, time, requester assessment and helper effort |
| RET-06 | R3 | Small purpose-based groups add value | Groups with a real shared intake or project; no empty-community demo | More resolved obstacles without disproportionate moderation |
| RET-07 | R3–R4 | Post-offer assistance preserves usefulness | Follow people choosing an onboarding or learning goal | Actual milestone support; changed stage recognised |
| RET-08 | R4 | Institutions accepting records strengthens reuse | Have a willing recipient process a record and request corrections | Repeat use in a real decision/workflow |

Set the observation period, recruitment method and decision threshold before each test in the evidence register. A handful of users informs design but does not justify general retention claims.

If a reminder improves return while increasing unwanted contact, it is not an unqualified win.

## 7. Honest growth loops

### Opportunity sharing

A person shares a current, useful listing. The recipient can read it and reach the original application route without an account.

Sign-up offers saved preparation and continuity. It should not retroactively lock information promised in the shared link.

### Evidence feedback

A person invites a chosen reviewer to inspect a work sample. The reviewer can understand the scope, respond or decline with low friction.

An invitation is not a licence to market to that reviewer or import their contact network.

### Peer help

A resolved answer can be made reusable with permission and appropriate redaction. Other people facing the same requirement find it useful.

Attach answers to source context and dates so old guidance does not become a permanent claim.

### Cohort invitation

A coordinator or peer brings a genuine group with a shared purpose. Participation remains optional and private records remain individually controlled.

Measure whether existing members benefit as relevant members join. Membership growth alone does not demonstrate a network effect.

### Accepted record

A receiving organisation successfully uses an export or share. It may choose a repeat workflow or introduce another team.

The growth event follows useful work; there is no implied institutional endorsement without agreement.

## 8. Channel sequence

| Release | Primary channels | Content or offer | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Founder-accessible apprentice peers and school-leaver contacts | Real demonstration and short observation session | Participation is voluntary; founder familiarity can bias feedback |
| R2 | User sharing, relevant community contacts, searchable opportunity pages | Accurate current listings and a useful preparation workspace | Source rights, quality and support readiness govern expansion |
| R3 | Small groups, mentors and willing programme coordinators | Specific help and evidence workflows | Real helpers and moderation must exist |
| R4 | Institutions, assessors and programme operators | Accepted records and demonstrated administrative value | Sales cycles and contracts need independent validation |
| R5 | Local partners and audiences in selected countries | Locally useful journeys and tested exports/integrations | Translate operations and requirements, not only copy |

The public launch remains independent of securing a school, employer or government contract.

No automatic posting to other people's groups, fabricated success stories, purchased testimonials or spam acquisition is permitted by this strategy.

## 9. Content quality and social design

Content should answer a real question, show actual work, explain a source-backed route or help someone act.

Separate public educational explanations from personalised advice based on incomplete facts.

Let users choose what achievements to share. Do not make rejection, unemployment or private educational information public by default.

Avoid employability leaderboards, popularity-based trust scores and rewards for submitting unnecessary applications.

A useful contribution can be an answer, review, clarification or thoughtful referral. Count its effect with the recipient's input.

A feed, if later justified, should offer controls, clear relevance and an end to the useful catch-up. Time spent is not the optimisation objective.

Scams, harassment, false credentials, impersonation and unsafe contact require the controls in [trust and safety](../security/10_TRUST_PRIVACY_AND_SAFETY.md) before release.

## 10. Operating the learning loop

After each supervised session, record what the person attempted, where they hesitated, what failed and what they actually completed.

Separate observation from interview opinion. A person saying they would use an app is not the same as returning unaided.

Invite criticism without pitching over it. Ask what they currently use and what would make changing tools worth the effort.

At sprint review, inspect at least one failed or abandoned journey alongside successful ones.

Keep feature experiments small enough to interpret, while the roadmap preserves the full vision. Do not launch multiple unrelated growth mechanisms and attribute all changes to one.

Use improvement evidence to sequence releases; retain long-term ideas in the backlog with explicit conditions rather than treating ambition as immediate demand.

## 11. Stop or redesign signals

Repeated irrelevant suggestions require better requirements/data before more notification volume.

Unresolved requests require helper supply or a narrower support promise before larger communities.

High preparation effort with little reuse requires simplifying record capture.

Positive interviews without voluntary use require revisiting the urgent problem and first experience.

Paid pilots that consume excessive support require changing scope, workflow or price before broad sales.

Stable users leaving after success should be distinguished from users leaving because the product failed.

## 12. Historical reconciliation

The original growth narrative centred on autonomous sharing, daily feeds, public employer scores and rapid premium conversion.

This plan retains sharing, community participation and persistent help while replacing unsupported viral claims with observable experiments.
