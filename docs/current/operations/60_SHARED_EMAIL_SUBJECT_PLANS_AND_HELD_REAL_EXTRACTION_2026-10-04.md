## Source-bound subjects without invented references

`recentjobs_programme_plan_v2` recognises the observed numbered sections: Available trades, Important requirements, Why applying is important, Why the two trades matter, and How to apply. It requires a bounded table with exactly two matched trade labels, each followed by one description paragraph; an exact source-written application email; an Attach section; two distinct matching `Trade — if applying for Trade` subject lines; and a closing-date section immediately afterwards.

This deliberately narrow layout rejects duplicate/mismatched subjects, absent or repeated trade labels, unmatched table rows, extra recipients, conflicting reference formats and unbounded application/document sections. Failure yields an unsupported plan with no partial intakes. Existing lettered programme plans with explicit references and separate emails retain their stable child identities.

Each new intake has an `application_subject` identity derived from the original notice identifier and normalised subject. Both application bindings retain original parent route index 0 and the shared three-line email evidence. Each subject retains its original value, complete conditional instruction and source span. Recruitment `reference` is null: a trade subject is not promoted into a reference code. Trade IDs remain stable across requirement revisions and change when their subject identity changes.

The full original numbered source stays visible to model review and stored evidence. Allowed evidence combines the introduction, selected trade block, shared requirement/application/document passages, that trade's subject and the closing section. It excludes the other trade's block/subject and general editorial career-benefit sections. Separated ranges are never concatenated into manufactured quotes. Short trade-title evidence is bound to the exact selected heading line, even if the same word appears in the shared introduction.
