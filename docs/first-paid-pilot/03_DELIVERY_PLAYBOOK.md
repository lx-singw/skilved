# First-pilot delivery playbook

Internal working guide for Linda. This service tests one commercial workflow alongside Skilved's six-category consumer MVP; it does not replace that product scope.

## Economics and capacity

The R1,500 price is an introductory experiment, not researched market pricing. Cap delivery at eight hours, including calls, coordination, reporting and one correction round. That is R187.50 per delivery hour before costs, sales time and tax if all eight hours are used. Record actual time; the effective rate falls when acquisition time is included.

Keep acquisition and demonstration spending at zero where possible. Use existing tools and a customer-controlled folder/register. No hosting purchase, paid advertising or new subscription is required to sell or deliver this pilot. Do not spend the customer's deposit as personal disposable income before delivering the work and accounting for costs.

For the first pilot, let the customer retain sensitive original documents. Work with learner reference IDs, status fields and redacted material wherever sufficient. Do not copy identity or financial records into public links, demonstration files or general AI chats. AI may help draft generic text or structure nonsensitive material; any processing of customer data needs an agreed purpose and suitable tools.

## Qualification: choose a customer with a current problem

Look for one independent provider or funded youth programme with:

- An existing cohort and an upcoming handover/reporting task.
- Repeated missing-item or supervisor-follow-up work.
- A coordinator willing to participate and a decision-maker who can approve R1,500.
- Authorised access to its records and a clear destination for the completed work.

Do not take a pilot whose success requires creating jobs, obtaining government API access, granting qualifications or replacing the customer's entire information system.

## Before accepting payment

Hold a 15-minute discovery call. Ask:

1. “Walk me through the last learner record that took too long to complete.”
2. “What exactly was missing, and where did your team look for it?”
3. “How often does this happen, and roughly how much staff time does it use?”
4. “What do your current tools already solve well?”
5. “Who owns the next cohort's records, and who can approve this pilot?”

Show the fictional demonstration. If the problem fits, offer the paid scope. A positive comment is not a sale. Record separately: interested, scope requested, scope agreed, payment requested, deposit received, delivered, balance received and repeat purchase.

Confirm the actual start date fits apprenticeship commitments. Promise a seven-working-day delivery window only when capacity and inputs are available. State expected response times; do not promise daytime live support you cannot provide.

## Delivery schedule

| Stage | Work | Budget | Output |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Day 1 | Confirm inputs and checklist; record starting statuses. | 1.5 hours | Baseline register and questions. |
| Day 2 | Identify missing items and prepare first consolidated follow-up. | 1 hour | Action queue and coordinator message drafts. |
| Days 3–4 | Process returned information; track who confirmed what. | 1.5 hours | Updated statuses with evidence references. |
| Day 5 | Prepare second follow-up for unresolved items only. | 0.5 hour | Second queue; identify delayed dependencies. |
| Day 6 | Prepare per-learner checklist and final summary. | 1.5 hours | Draft handover pack. |
| Day 7 | Handover, coordinator review and billing milestone. | 1 hour | Delivered pack and remaining actions. |
| Review window | One round of corrections. | 1 hour | Corrected pack, if needed. |

Total maximum planned delivery effort: eight hours. Waiting for responses is tracked separately from work time. If responses do not arrive, report the item as outstanding rather than extending work indefinitely or filling it with assumptions.

## Register template

Use this in the customer's existing spreadsheet or document:

| Learner reference | Requirement | Initial status | Current status | Evidence reference | Confirmed by / date | Next action | Owner | Due date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [agreed item] | [status] | [status] | [customer-owned reference] | [attribution] | [action] | [owner] | [date] |

Statuses: present, missing, needs clarification, waiting for confirmation, not applicable with reason. Record authenticity/assessment separately if the provider has a genuine basis for it. Do not call an entire learner “verified.”

- Ending position: same categories, with changes reconciled.

## Payment and repeat work

Request the balance when the agreed deliverables meet acceptance criteria. Do not promise that payments from a customer's funder will follow. After handover ask: “Would you pay to use this for the next cohort? What would need to improve?” Obtain a separate scope and price for repeat work.

Permission to use any testimonial, organisation name or anonymised results must be explicit and separate. A completed pilot is not permission to publish learner information or claim an institutional partnership.

## Stop or change the offer when

- The existing system already solves the task and there is no meaningful residual work.
- No one owns the task or budget.
- Actual work consistently exceeds the time allowance at the proposed fee.
- Buyers want a different repeated deliverable; revise the offer using their evidence.

Do not build a new dashboard just because prospects praise the idea. Build after paid delivery reveals repeated work worth automating.
