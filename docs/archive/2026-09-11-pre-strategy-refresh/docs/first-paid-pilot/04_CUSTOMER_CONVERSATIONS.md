# Customer conversation kit

Prepared drafts only. Nothing in this kit has been sent, and no customer, booking or payment is implied.

## First message to a relevant contact

> Hi [name], I'm Linda, building Skilved. I'm offering a small hands-on pilot for training providers: organising incomplete learner records and preparing follow-ups so the coordinator can see exactly what is missing and who needs to act.
>
> It covers ten records, five agreed checks and a final progress report for R1,500. I have a five-minute fictional example to show the output. Does your team have a current cohort where this work is taking too much time?

For a warm contact, begin with your real connection. Do not imply a referral or institutional relationship that does not exist.

## If they ask whether this is working software

> Skilved is in development. This pilot is a defined, hands-on service using your existing tools, with me responsible for the agreed deliverables. The demonstration shows the output format using fictional records. I am testing whether the service solves a problem worth paying for before expanding the software.

## After a useful discovery call

> Thanks for explaining [specific problem they actually described]. Based on that, I propose reviewing [up to ten] records against [agreed checks], preparing two consolidated follow-up lists for [coordinator], and handing over an updated register and outstanding-action report.
>
> The total pilot fee is R1,500: R750 deposit once we agree the scope/start date, and R750 when the agreed deliverables are accepted. Would you like me to send the written scope for [proposed start date]?

## A single follow-up

> Hi [name], following up on the learner-record pilot. Is this a current priority for your team? If so, we can agree the ten-record scope. If timing or fit is wrong, a quick indication would help me avoid chasing you unnecessarily.

Use the person's preferred channel and honour a no or no-contact request. Do not send repeated automated messages.

## Common objections

**“We already have a learner-management system.”**

> That makes sense. Does your team still spend time getting people to provide missing items or confirmations? The pilot can work with your current register. If your system already handles that well, this may not be useful.

**“Can you guarantee all records will be complete?”**

> I can commit to the review, two follow-up rounds and accurate handover. I cannot guarantee someone will supply a missing item. Every unresolved item will have its status, owner and next action recorded.

**“Can we try it for free?”**

> I can show the fictional demonstration and discuss one redacted example on a short call. The ten-record delivery is paid because it includes hands-on work. We can agree a smaller paid scope if that is more appropriate.

**“Can you verify qualifications or guarantee a placement?”**

> This pilot organises evidence and follow-up. Your authorised reviewers and receiving organisations retain qualification, assessment and placement decisions.

**“Why R1,500?”**

> It is an introductory fixed fee for a tightly defined first pilot, including the review, follow-ups, report and correction round. We can judge the value against your actual staff effort and the usefulness of the handover.

## Ten-contact experiment

Start with people you can reach legitimately: existing contacts, a provider's published business contact, or a genuine introduction. Prioritise independent providers with a live cohort and an accessible programme manager. A large platform partnership is not required.

Aim for ten relevant, individually considered contacts and three discovery conversations. These are activity targets, not expected conversion rates. Seek one paid scope. Record what happened:

Do not populate this with invented leads or count promised payments as received revenue. After ten relevant contacts, review the objections and adjust targeting, scope or pricing before building more features.

## First actions for Linda

1. Read the offer and check that you can genuinely deliver its eight-hour workload around your apprenticeship.
2. Rehearse the demonstration once using only its fictional records.
3. Choose three reachable providers or programme contacts; identify a real coordinator/budget owner.
4. Personalise the first message and send it yourself, or separately authorise assistance with sending.
5. On the first call, understand the current process before demonstrating.
6. Ask for a paid pilot, then confirm scope and accurate payment details before accepting money.
