# Vertical unbundling: historical limits and three contested opportunities

Research cut-off: 14 September 2026. Scope: South Africa first, with a venture-scale international ambition. Facts below are supported by primary sources; proposed wedges, pricing tests, scores and expansion routes are analytical hypotheses, not demonstrated demand. Competitor websites establish advertised capabilities, not verified performance. No source supplies more than approximately 150 words of source-derived prose in this memo.

## The historical analogy is useful, but the causal claim is too strong

WhatsApp did not originate as a feature spun out of Facebook. In its February 2014 acquisition announcement, WhatsApp described an independently built communication service with 450 million monthly users, built in under five years. Facebook explicitly described a cross-platform mobile messaging company that would retain its brand and operate independently. This supports a functional comparison—dedicated mobile communication versus a broad social network—but does not establish that WhatsApp's founders identified and extracted Facebook messaging. [1][2]

Airbnb's IPO filing locates its origin in hosting three guests in the founders' San Francisco home in 2007, then designing trust between strangers in 2008. Its model added booking, payment and trust infrastructure to accommodation discovery. “Unbundled Craigslist housing” therefore compresses a much larger change: creating a transaction system and expanding available accommodation supply. Tinder's own account says it began on a university campus in 2012. Neither source establishes the simplistic proposition that these companies were deliberate feature extractions from Craigslist. [3][4]

Vertical unbundling is a recognised strategic lens. Jordan and Coolican's original 2019 a16z essay describes entrants specialising in a platform category and tailoring the experience or model to that category. Their concrete example is StubHub's ticket verification, venue maps and fulfilment against eBay. The lesson is to identify a valuable outcome a general platform cannot serve well with a common product—not to find a tab, copy it, and assume distribution follows. [5]

Three moves should be distinguished. **Functional unbundling** extracts a job such as messaging from a broader application. **Verticalisation** serves a particular industry or participant with specialised data, workflow and economics. **Rebundling** then assembles adjacent activities around that customer's record. They can coexist, but require different advantages. A care handover tool can become a care operations platform; a generic chat clone has no corresponding claim on patient administration or payments.

There is direct recent evidence for rebundling: nCino said its March 2024 acquisition of DocFox would combine document collection, due diligence and business account opening with its banking platform. Its filing subsequently records completion on 20 March and a preliminary purchase price of $74.3 million. This is a credible narrower-company outcome, but it is not evidence that every specialised compliance tool becomes an independent unicorn. [6][7]

## Why the narrow product can lose

These are strategic failure hypotheses to test, not statistical failure rates. A feature may matter intensely once a year but fail to support recurring spend. Its user may not control a budget. The incumbent's broader package may be “good enough” at no additional price. A fresh interface may eliminate ten minutes while creating an hour of duplicate data entry. A marketplace may secure registrations without enough geographically and temporally relevant supply to complete transactions. Participants may meet once, then transact outside the platform. AI may make the initial feature cheaper for everyone, including incumbents.

The relevant test is a **complete narrow outcome**: a new customer can finish a valuable job using the product with existing counterparties and existing records. That avoids demanding a new network before delivering value. Expansion should follow the record created by this outcome, and the founder should identify a second and third budget that the same record unlocks. “More features later” is not a venture thesis.

An important portfolio implication follows: diversify discovery experiments, not necessarily operating companies. Several ventures run simultaneously by one founder share the same scarce sales attention, capital and execution capacity. Three short paid-pilot searches followed by concentrated execution is a more credible experiment portfolio than three unfinished platforms.

## Opportunity 1: workplace competency evidence, beginning with one occupation

**Facts and existing competition.** QCTO's official FAQs require provider accreditation documents including workplace agreements and staff qualification evidence, and specify learner-enrolment reporting and external assessment requirements. This is an administratively structured system with real records and deadlines. But the generic “QCTO evidence platform” is already occupied: skillSYMS advertises learner records, evidence portfolios, assessment, workplace logbooks and statutory exports; Yiba Verified advertises learner lifecycle, attendance, logbooks and QCTO readiness; Learnership Manager advertises attendance, task evidence and supervisor/assessor sign-offs. [8][9][10][11]

**Plausible wedge—hypothesis.** Select a single occupation with distributed workplaces, expensive supervisor follow-up and an accessible anchor employer. Build the sequence from a learner's task evidence to supervisor acceptance, exception resolution and an assessment-ready export. Begin as a companion to the provider's existing system. Sell reduced missing-evidence rework and faster verified completion, rather than “an AI LMS”. Assessment decisions and qualifications must remain with authorised institutions and people; software should preserve source evidence and human accountability.

The buyer could be the training-provider operations director or employer learning manager; avoid a first model funded by learners. Interview both because the provider may suffer the reporting burden while the employer controls workplace evidence. Acquire the first accounts through assessors, training consultants and one employer's provider network. A sensible initial test is three paying providers working with the same qualification, rather than many unrelated curricula.

**Expansion and defence—hypothesis.** Progress from evidence capture to an employer-accepted competency record, expiry/renewal management, deployment eligibility and workforce training procurement. For international expansion, target the same occupational workflow and multinational employer before translating national accreditation rules. The desirable asset is verified provenance and repeated employer use, not a pile of learner documents. A network effect exists only if additional accepted issuers and employers increase actual reuse; certificates sitting in separate customer silos are not a network.

Credly already offers employer skills visibility and verified credential infrastructure; Sitepass already sells contractor qualification, training and site-readiness workflows. These are expansion competitors, not merely distant comparables. [12][13] A South African QCTO export utility could be a sound small business. A venture-scale thesis requires demonstrated cross-employer reuse and larger workforce budgets. Stop or reposition if every new provider needs bespoke templates, the product overlaps the existing LMS without replacing meaningful work, or employers will not accept its evidence.

**Pilot decision rule—proposed:** obtain payment from at least three organisations, reduce administrative handling time by at least 30% against a measured baseline, and secure one employer willing to reuse accepted evidence across two providers. These thresholds are management choices, not industry benchmarks.

## Opportunity 2: home-care handover and visit-to-invoice administration

**Facts and existing competition.** Stats SA estimates 6.6 million South Africans aged 60+ in 2025, versus 3.6 million in 2002; only 21.9% of older people had medical-aid coverage in 2024. Ageing establishes a need context, not the number of software-buying care agencies or families. [14] Birdie already combines care management, rostering, finance, compliance and family access; local-facing MedGuardian advertises senior-care software, while Sikhaya Health advertises case management, scheduling and referral intake. A “WhatsApp for carers” is therefore insufficient differentiation. [15][16][17]

**Plausible wedge—hypothesis.** Begin with independent private-pay home-care agencies in one metro. Solve a coordinator's missed handover and incomplete-visit-record problem: capture visit completion, structured observations, an accountable escalation, supervisor closure and a billing-ready record. Family updates should be a consequence of reliable operations rather than the sole product. Avoid entering first through hospital procurement, claiming clinical outcomes, or building medication recommendations.

The economic buyer is the agency owner or operations manager; carers are critical users and families are beneficiaries. Recruit through a respected nurse manager and agency referrals. The first trial should run alongside current processes with clearly assigned escalation responsibility. Offline capture, role-specific disclosure, audit history and incident recovery matter more than a conversational UI. Health data is special personal information under POPIA, with specific authorisations and processing conditions; consent alone should not be assumed to settle the design. [18]

**Expansion and defence—hypothesis.** If visit data is reliable, extend into roster optimisation, payroll and invoices; then insurer or hospital referral coordination where partnerships justify the added integration and clinical governance. International expansion follows countries with similar agency operating models but requires separate reimbursement and professional-rule work. Embedded daily workflow, trusted integrations and measured coordination outcomes could become defensible. “We own sensitive data” is neither sufficient nor an appropriate moat claim.

Birdie disclosed a $30 million Series B in June 2022, supporting the proposition that investors have financed comprehensive home-care infrastructure, not that this South African wedge has demand or that the category is open. [19] A family-update subscription alone is more plausibly a niche business. A cross-country agency operating platform has venture potential but severe incumbent and implementation risk. Stop if agencies want custom outsourcing rather than reusable software, frontline completion remains poor, or buyers cannot demonstrate economic benefit independent of unproven clinical claims.

**Pilot decision rule—proposed:** two paying agencies, four weeks of sustained caregiver use, a 30% reduction in coordinator handling time, and reconciliation of completed visits to invoices without extra data entry. Record complaints and missed escalations as equal decision inputs to time savings.

## Opportunity 3: AML compliance exceptions for a narrowly defined SME network

**Facts and existing competition.** The FIC's 2024/25 annual report states that 556 risk-based inspections produced 330 reports requiring remediation. Because inspections were risk-based, 330/556 cannot be treated as the non-compliance rate of all South African firms. The PPRA identifies developing, implementing and maintaining a risk-management and compliance programme as an estate-agency obligation. These support a real recurring operational problem, not a universal demand estimate. [20][21]

Competition is formidable. DocFox now redirects to nCino KYC Africa, which advertises local onboarding, watchlist screening, configurable risk ratings, account verification, expert advice and training, with solutions for law firms, property and high-value goods dealers. “FICA document uploads plus AI” is a direct incumbent collision. [22]

**Plausible wedge—hypothesis.** Work with compliance consultants serving one narrow segment—for example small property franchise offices—and discover unresolved exceptions across existing tools. Build consultant-to-office case ownership: missing beneficial-ownership evidence, review deadlines, documented decisions, remedial tasks and inspection exports. Integrate specialist screening and identity vendors instead of recreating their datasets. The customer receives an evidence trail; qualified people retain legal interpretation and acceptance decisions.

A consultant channel could aggregate small accounts and lower distribution cost, but consultant willingness to standardise must be proven. A compliance professional should be a domain partner before launch. Price per active institution or managed portfolio, with verification costs visible. Do not assume the regulator's maximum possible fine equals willingness to pay.

**Expansion and defence—hypothesis.** Move from one firm's case management into a multi-institution evidence and review network, with permissioned reuse and jurisdiction-specific policy packs. Cross-border portability concerns underlying entity facts and provenance; an institution's legal responsibility and risk decision do not automatically transfer. Becoming a generic global compliance suite from day one would multiply legal and integration costs before product-market fit.

This has the best apparent urgency of the three, but the weakest easy product differentiation. A regional consultancy-plus-software business can be valuable. An independent venture platform requires demonstrable distribution through networks, repeatable product delivery and retention beyond a one-off compliance scare. Reject if incumbent suites already close the exact exception workflow, if every sale requires bespoke legal work, or if the only advantage is lower price.

**Pilot decision rule—proposed:** a consultant introduces five firms, at least three pay, case-resolution time improves materially against actual prior cases, and the second consultant can onboard without founder-led customisation. Also compare a live incumbent demo against the same workflow before building.

## Comparative judgment

| Candidate | Attractive entry condition | Venture-scale prerequisite | Main reason to reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competency evidence | Access to one employer-provider cluster | Reusable accepted evidence across employers and countries | National-reporting utility with no external reuse |
| Care administration | A trusted agency/nurse-manager partner | Full visit-to-payment operations across many agencies | Frontline adoption and strong integrated competitors |
| Compliance exceptions | A consultant network with repeated unresolved cases | Multi-institution distribution and portable evidence infrastructure | Incumbent coverage and expert-services cost |

None warrants an immediate broad build. Training is the most accessible of these if there is founder proximity to providers; care has the clearest daily operational frequency; compliance has the most explicit regulated urgency. Those are conditional judgments. The next investment should purchase access to actual workflows, paid pilots and competitor comparisons. It should not purchase a large speculative codebase.
