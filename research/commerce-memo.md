# Commerce and professional-workflow unbundling opportunities

Research cut-off: 14 September 2026. South Africa first, global expansion, venture ambition. All prices are source-currency list prices observed on that date, not quotations or exchange-rate conversions. Recommendations, proposed prices, experimental thresholds, and scale scenarios below are hypotheses; no customer interviews or willingness-to-pay tests were conducted.

## Comparative judgment

The strongest opening is to take responsibility for an economically important object currently scattered across messages: a distributor order, a contractor's promised part, or an export shipment's evidence pack. Chat is the input channel. Reliable transaction state, exceptions, accountability, and integrations are the product. A generic WhatsApp bot or nicer portal is already readily substitutable.

|---|---|---|---|---|

### 1. Distributor post-delivery claims: a narrower hypothesis

**Candidate beachhead:** a distributor with enough recurring shortage, damage, price or return disputes to occupy finance/operations staff. Start with distributors, not individual spaza shops: one payer can bring an existing customer base. Which subvertical has sufficient claim volume remains a hypothesis requiring access to remittances, invoices, delivery records and credit notes.

The competitive baseline rules out a broad ordering pitch. Choco's 8 July 2026 documentation covers emailed orders and forwarded WhatsApp screenshots, messages and voice notes. Orderspace offers a B2B ordering site from £39/month excluding VAT. [C1][C2] More decisively, Cape Town's Brand2Market advertises POS integration, customer-specific pricing, AI order extraction and manual review, including packaging customers. It publishes 0.3% of WhatsApp sales, a R3,000/month floor and R10,000 ceiling, with setup included and Meta fees separate. OrderMint also describes controlled SKU/unit, credit, warehouse and dispatch workflows. These capabilities cannot be presented as competitive whitespace. [C15][C16]

**Wedge to investigate:** a short payment or damaged-goods claim becomes a structured case linked to the original order, invoice, proof of delivery, agreed price and correspondence. The tool assembles evidence, identifies a missing document, routes approval and tracks the eventual credit or recovered balance. It should complement ordering systems. This too has global competitors: Esker specifically offers deduction workflows for shortages, damages and price discrepancies, with evidence retrieval and ERP links. The hypothesis is a poorly served South African customer tier or category—not absence of software. [C17]

**Distribution and validation:** recruit through accounting/ERP implementation partners and distributor finance referrals. Shadow three claims desks and examine 50–100 historical cases each with permission. Measure cases/month, staff minutes/case, missing evidence, aging, invalid deductions and eventual resolution. Run a human-reviewed parallel service before posting accounting entries. A proposed R5,000–R15,000/month pilot is an experiment, not observed willingness to pay; compare staff savings and incremental recovery against the complete cost. Use customer-controlled workflows for correspondence and approvals.

**Kill criteria:** stop if fewer than three of ten qualified firms accept a paid pilot; claims are too rare; incumbent modules solve the problem at lower switching cost; or reviewed case preparation fails to fall at least 50%. Recovery rates require longer follow-up and a comparison cohort, not attribution of every collected rand to the tool. Reject if evidence access requires unsupported portal scraping or if integrations never repeat. Conduct a direct feature comparison with Brand2Market, the customer's ERP and enterprise receivables vendors before building.

**Expansion:** claims evidence → returns/credit-note authorization → cash application and receivables reconciliation → broader order-to-cash operations. Customer-specific histories and integrations can create switching costs; a model alone does not. Cross-party records may eventually improve collaboration, but access to private data is not an automatic network effect. Financing is a separate capability, not inevitable monetization. Avoid inventory or credit exposure to compensate for weak software demand.

**South African caution:** Yebo Fresh currently markets a technology-plus-field-distribution operation and claims relationships with 72,000 store owners and 180 midi wholesalers, supported by 220 field staff. These are company claims, not an audited market census. They demonstrate that reaching informal retailers can be a substantial physical operation. A lean software company should test a distributor-led acquisition model rather than assume cheap viral distribution. [C3]

**Venture test:** an illustrative 5,000 businesses at US$1,000/month produces US$60 million annual subscription revenue. This is a required scale condition, not TAM or a forecast. South African adoption alone does not establish a globally reachable paying population. Before fundraising around it, demonstrate repeatable onboarding, measurable recovery/efficiency and expansion revenue within one vertical. A small claims desk business is useful, but venture scale remains unproven.

### 2. Procurement exceptions for commercial refrigeration/HVAC contractors

**Candidate beachhead:** contractors maintaining commercial refrigeration or HVAC equipment across multiple sites. Unbundle supplier quote comparison, missing confirmations, promised delivery dates, substitutions and receipt matching from email, spreadsheets and job-management systems. The buyer is the contractor's operations/procurement leader; suppliers participate through an email or lightweight link, initially free.

Competition already includes full procurement suites. Tradecloud's published Starter package is €998/month, billed annually, plus implementation/ERP integration and a prepaid transaction tariff. Its product includes order collaboration and exception handling. Commusoft already creates supplier purchase orders, tracks parts through stock and vehicles, and links procurement to job costing. Thus “purchase orders for trades” is insufficient differentiation. [C4][C5]

**Wedge:** for each live job, show exactly which ordered part is unconfirmed, late, short-delivered or differently priced. Extract proposed supplier updates into a review queue and link them to the original PO. Do not initially choose safety-critical substitute parts: let a qualified employee authorize compatibility. The economic proposition is fewer stalled jobs and fewer unbilled purchases, with administrative savings as a measurable early benefit.

**Go-to-market:** a founder should work with five contractors sharing a supplier category and one job/accounting stack. Offer to reconcile one week of open POs manually. Sell a live exceptions desk only if the retrospective reveals missed commitments with financial consequences. Proposed pilots: R5,000–R15,000/month depending on volume and sites; establish value from customer records before promising savings. Tradecloud's enterprise-oriented economics are evidence of budgets, not proof South African contractors will pay these proposed amounts.

**Kill criteria:** abandon if supplier confirmations cannot be obtained without coercing suppliers onto a new system; staff refuse to record receipts; or incumbent software configuration eliminates the measured problem. Reject a mere email summarizer: it should connect a discrepancy to a job, responsible person and next action. Stop if implementation remains a consulting engagement after several similar customers.

**Platform route:** category-specific quote/availability data → reliable supplier performance history → repeat replenishment → procurement transactions → installed-asset service planning. A larger platform could coordinate contractors, distributors and equipment owners. This requires current, permissioned data and sufficient category density; merely storing private purchase orders does not create a network effect. A narrow exception desk could be an excellent profitable SaaS business while remaining too small for venture returns.

### 3. Export shipment evidence orchestration

**Candidate beachhead:** one commodity and trade corridor, acquired through an export manager or specialist forwarding desk. The opportunity is the work surrounding official systems: requesting missing evidence, checking consistency among shipment documents, tracking versions and approvals, and assembling a handover pack. It is not a substitute certificate authority.

South Africa already has digital export certification. The government's eCertification page describes online plant/plant-product phytosanitary applications and status tracking. eCert documents an API, but client systems must register; its published application endpoint notes that it is reserved for fruit. The real accessibility and current commodity scope need direct technical confirmation before product commitments. [C6][C7]

Generic document generation is also occupied: IncoDocs displays Basic at $29/month for 30 documents and Professional at $74/month for 100, with billing toggles on its pricing page; exact commitment and tax treatment require confirmation. It already synchronizes information across shipping-document sets. South African incumbent Paltrack supplies exporter/agent software and claims its D2 service switches more than 70% of fruit-industry logistical information. This claim signals incumbent integration depth rather than a verified independent market share. [C8][C9][C10]

**Wedge:** choose a narrowly evidenced gap, such as resolving inconsistencies between a packing list, booking reference and certificate application before a specialist approves submission. Build a shipment record with field-level provenance, required-document status, named owners and deadlines. Preserve original documents and record every change. Rules must identify the jurisdiction, commodity and effective date. A professional reviews regulatory judgments; AI drafts and highlights discrepancies.

**Entry and pricing experiment:** partner with a domain practitioner and recruit three firms in the same corridor. Review 30 completed shipment packs, then monitor a new cohort prospectively. Measure preparation time, preventable corrections and time awaiting documents. Charge a proposed R5,000–R20,000/month or a documented per-shipment fee only after confirming recurring volume and value. Do not promise reduced port delays when the cause is outside document preparation.

**Kill criteria:** no repeatable cross-company gap; required API/data access denied; customers primarily want bespoke advisory work; official/incumbent workflows already capture the information; or too little shipment frequency outside peak season. The strongest product may integrate with Paltrack/eCert instead of displacing them.

**Venture route:** start with a repeated shipment evidence object, then expand to adjacent commodities/corridors, counterpart collaboration, freight milestones and receivables documentation. A global platform is plausible if the same data model travels and country-specific rules can be maintained economically. If each corridor requires a separate consulting team, it is a services business. Avoid claiming that all exports need identical documents: SARS explicitly links documentary requirements to the relevant circumstances. [C11]

### 4. Print/packaging approval-to-production: conditional, lower priority

**Candidate beachhead:** commercial printers or packaging producers whose customers approve artwork informally but later dispute the production specification, quantity or version. Extract one completed approval into a versioned production release, with authorized approver, print specifications, price-affecting changes and final file attached.

Generic client approvals are crowded. Ziflow offers a free plan with unlimited proofs/reviewers and two users; Standard is $199/month billed annually. Filestage lists a free tier and Starter at $199/month, and permits reviewers without accounts. Neither “free reviewers” nor “one approval link” differentiates a new entrant. [C12][C13]

**Wedge and distribution:** connect approved artwork to the actual production job and commercial change order. Partner with print consultants or a print-management reseller; ask to examine ten recent reprints or disputed changes. A proposed R1,000–R4,000/month pilot must reduce preventable rework or collect previously unbilled revisions. Kill if existing proofing tools plus a checklist solve the problem, buyers value only cheaper pricing, or external clients still approve the wrong version outside the record.

**Scale judgment:** attractive as a focused small business, weak as a standalone venture thesis. Venture potential requires expanding into production coordination, estimating, specifications, supplier collaboration and repeat purchasing. That is a materially larger undertaking with established incumbents. Do not make it a priority merely because it can be built quickly.

## Common platform constraints and next action

Official WhatsApp pricing varies by recipient market and message category. The current official page describes a delivery-based message model and service-window exceptions. Treat messaging/provider costs and policy changes as variable costs; keep the order/shipment record usable through email and browser links. This is a channel dependency, not a defensible moat. [C14]

Prioritize contractor procurement discovery, with distributor claims as a second experiment only where the founder can access transaction evidence. The first decisive asset is access to real workflows and paying design partners. Public evidence establishes categories and competitive bars; it does not establish unmet South African demand. Local ordering competition is substantial, so no South African pricing, trust or distribution advantage should be assumed. Do not launch four products simultaneously in response to four plausible hypotheses.
